<h2><span class="label">I · January–July 2014</span>The road south</h2>

The advance on Sanaa began in the far north. In January 2014, after months of siege and fighting, the Salafi students and families of the Dar al-Hadith institute in Dammaj, Saada governorate, were evacuated under a government-mediated deal.<sup><a href="#s4">4</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

Weeks later the Houthis defeated tribal forces of the al-Ahmar family, leaders of the Hashid confederation, in Amran governorate. On 8 July they took the city of Amran itself, about 50 kilometres north of the capital. The army's 310th Armoured Brigade was overrun and its commander, Brigadier General Hamid al-Qushaibi, was killed.<sup><a href="#s4">4</a></sup><sup><a href="#s6">6</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

The political process was moving at the same time. The National Dialogue Conference closed in January 2014, and a plan to divide Yemen into six federal regions followed in February. The Houthis rejected it. The plan placed Saada in a region with no access to the sea.<sup><a href="#s5">5</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

<h2><span class="label">II · July–September 2014</span>The fuel protests</h2>

On 30 July the government cut fuel subsidies and prices rose sharply. In August, Abdulmalik al-Houthi called for mass protests demanding that the increase be reversed and the government resign. Supporters set up sit-ins inside Sanaa, and armed camps appeared at the entrances to the city.<sup><a href="#s7">7</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

President Hadi dismissed the government in early September and offered a partial price cut. The Houthis rejected the offer. On 9 September, security forces fired on protesters near the cabinet offices and several people were killed.<sup><a href="#s7">7</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

<h2><span class="label">III · 16–21 September 2014</span>Six days of fighting</h2>

Fighting broke out in the north-west of the capital between Houthi fighters and forces loyal to General Ali Mohsen al-Ahmar, backed by armed supporters of the Islah party. The clashes centred on the First Armoured Division headquarters and al-Iman University.<sup><a href="#s7">7</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

On 21 September the Houthis took the division's headquarters, the army command, ministries and state television. Ali Mohsen left the country and later appeared in Saudi Arabia. Prime Minister Mohammed Basindawa resigned the same day.<sup><a href="#s7">7</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

That evening, at the presidential palace, the Houthis and the main parties signed the Peace and National Partnership Agreement, brokered by the UN envoy Jamal Benomar. It provided for a new government, a lower fuel price, and the withdrawal of armed groups from the capital once security was restored.<sup><a href="#s3">3</a></sup> The Houthis did not withdraw. <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

<div class="both" role="note" aria-label="Two names for the same day">
  <div><span class="label">The movement calls it</span><p>The 21 September Revolution, marked each year as a national holiday in areas under its control.</p></div>
  <div><span class="label">Its opponents call it</span><p>The coup of 21 September, the term used by the internationally recognised government and its allies.</p></div>

<h2><span class="label">IV · Why the army stood aside</span>The Saleh factor</h2>

Former President Ali Abdullah Saleh had fought six wars against the Houthis. By 2014 he was working with them. Units loyal to him did not defend the capital. In November 2014 the UN Security Council placed sanctions on Saleh and two senior Houthi commanders, Abd al-Khaliq al-Houthi and Abdullah Yahya al-Hakim, for threatening Yemen's peace and stability.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup><sup><a href="#s2">2</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

<h2><span class="label">V · After 21 September</span>The state from inside</h2>

Armed "popular committees" took up positions across the city, and Houthi supervisors were placed inside ministries and state institutions. A new prime minister, Khaled Bahah, formed a government in November.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

The arrangement lasted four months. In January 2015 the Houthis seized the presidential palace and President Hadi resigned. In February they issued a "constitutional declaration" dissolving parliament, and Hadi escaped to Aden. On 26 March 2015 a Saudi-led coalition began airstrikes.
