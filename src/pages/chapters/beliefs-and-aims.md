<h2><span class="label">I · The name</span>A march, a leader and a project</h2>

The movement's own website describes the Quranic March as resting on three pillars: "the umma, the leadership, and the project" (*الأمة، والقيادة، والمشروع*). It refers to its founder as "the Martyr Leader" (*الشهيد القائد*) Hussein Badr al-Din al-Houthi, and to his brother Abdulmalik as "the Master Leader" (*السيد القائد*).<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Official source</span>

The pillars are the umma, the whole Muslim community, not Yemen. The movement does not describe itself as a Yemeni national party. <span class="ev-tag">◇ Analysis</span>

<h2><span class="label">II · 2012</span>Chosen by God</h2>

In February 2012 the movement issued its Intellectual and Cultural Document (*الوثيقة الفكرية والثقافية*), drawn up under Abdulmalik al-Houthi. According to the researcher Ahmad al-Dughashi, who quotes it, the document states:<sup><a href="#s2">2</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Quoted by a named researcher</span>

<div class="both" role="note" aria-label="Quotation">
  <div><span class="label">Arabic</span><p lang="ar" dir="rtl">«ونعتقد أن الله سبحانه اصطفى أهل بيت رسول الله»</p></div>
  <div><span class="label">Translation</span><p>"We believe that God, glory be to Him, chose the household of the Messenger of God."</p></div>
</div>

The same document names Ali ibn Abi Talib as the rightful imam after the Prophet.<sup><a href="#s2">2</a></sup> The Middle East Institute's Nadwa Al-Dawsari reads the document as holding that only the Prophet's descendants, known in Yemen as the *sadah* or Hashemites, may rule Muslims. She notes that Abdulmalik carries the title *'alam al-huda*, "the banner of guidance", and is described as owed unconditional obedience.<sup><a href="#s3">3</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">◇ Analysis by a named researcher</span>

<h2><span class="label">III · 2000–2002</span>The slogan</h2>

Every Houthi gathering since the early 2000s has chanted the *sarkha*, the "scream", which the movement says Hussein al-Houthi first voiced in 2002:<sup><a href="#s4">4</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

<div class="both" role="note" aria-label="The slogan">
  <div><span class="label">Arabic</span><p lang="ar" dir="rtl">الله أكبر، الموت لأمريكا، الموت لإسرائيل، اللعنة على اليهود، النصر للإسلام</p></div>
  <div><span class="label">Translation</span><p>"God is great. Death to America. Death to Israel. A curse on the Jews. Victory for Islam."</p></div>
</div>

The Sana'a Center's study of Houthi media found that the slogan was first a call to arms. Once the movement ruled from Sanaa, its media recast it as a pan-Arab and pan-Islamic message with aims reaching beyond Yemen.<sup><a href="#s4">4</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

<h2><span class="label">IV · The lectures</span>A break with Zaydi tradition</h2>

Hussein al-Houthi's lectures, printed as the *Malazem*, are the movement's core curriculum. An index of quotations from them, compiled by the Dorar al-Saniyya Foundation, a Salafi publisher opposed to the movement, attributes these words to him:<sup><a href="#s5">5</a></sup><sup><a href="#s6">6</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">◇ Quoted by an opposing source · original being checked</span>

- On Zaydi scholarship, from *مسؤولية طلاب العلوم الدينية*: Zaydis live "in a state of humiliation worse than that inflicted on the Children of Israel".
- On past Zaydi imams, from *سورة المائدة*, lesson 2: "imams appeared, even within Zaydism, who were not fit to rule the umma".
- On Khomeini, from *خطر دخول أمريكا اليمن*: "Imam Khomeini was a just imam, a pious imam".

Taken together, these passages show a founder who rejected much of the Zaydi scholarly tradition he came from, and looked to the Iranian revolution as a model. Zaydi scholars who opposed him made the same criticism. <span class="ev-tag">◇ Analysis</span>

<h2><span class="label">V · 2020–2022</span>Belief becomes law</h2>

Once in power in Sanaa, the movement wrote these beliefs into the rules of the state. In 2020 its authorities issued zakat regulations that assign a fifth (*khums*) of revenue from certain resources to the descendants of Bani Hashim. The Sana'a Center called this a move to institutionalise a Hashemite elite. Mwatana for Human Rights said it institutionalised discrimination among Yemenis.<sup><a href="#s7">7</a></sup><sup><a href="#s8">8</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

In November 2022 the movement approved a code of conduct for state employees. It treats government work as a form of worship, names the leader's speeches and lectures among its references, and requires attendance at cultural courses built on Hussein al-Houthi's lectures.<sup><a href="#s9">9</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

<h2><span class="label">VI · Mecca and Saudi Arabia</span>What they say, and what is alleged</h2>

In June 2024, a Houthi Hajj delegation led by Yahya al-Razami, head of the movement's military council, was filmed in Mecca pledging allegiance to God, the Prophet, Imam Ali, and then to Abdulmalik al-Houthi.<sup><a href="#s10">10</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

Abdulmalik al-Houthi has repeatedly attacked Saudi Arabia's management of the Hajj, accusing it of turning the pilgrimage into "a nightmare of obstacles" and of using it for political ends.<sup><a href="#s14">14</a></sup> On 16 June 2026 he declared the movement ready for combat against what he called more than eleven years of Saudi aggression and blockade.<sup><a href="#s11">11</a></sup> In a speech in August 2026 he called Saudi Arabia "the Kingdom of the Devil's Horn", accused it of opening "the airspace of the Two Holy Sanctuaries" to Israeli flights, and called for action "by all legitimate means" to end its "tyranny".<sup><a href="#s12">12</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

Saudi Arabia has twice accused the Houthis of targeting Mecca itself: with a ballistic missile on 27 July 2017, and with a drone it said it shot down on 15 September 2026. The Houthis denied both, saying they target only military and oil sites.<sup><a href="#s13">13</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">◇ Allegation · denied</span>

<h2><span class="label">VII · Beyond Yemen</span>The wider project</h2>

During the battle for Marib in 2021, the senior Houthi official Mohammed al-Bukhaiti called the city "the gate to Jerusalem" and said the movement was "implementing the will and orders of God".<sup><a href="#s3">3</a></sup> From November 2023 the movement attacked commercial shipping in the Red Sea. In 2026 it took part in Iran's war with Israel and the United States, declared a blockade of Saudi oil exports, and seized Yemen's Red Sea coast and the islands of the Bab al-Mandab strait.<sup><a href="#s11">11</a></sup><sup><a href="#s15">15</a></sup> <span class="ev-tag">■ Documented</span>

The pattern this record traces is one of words followed by action. A slogan aimed at America and Israel became a campaign against shipping in the Red Sea. A doctrine that God chose one family to lead became law in the khums regulations. Rhetoric against Saudi Arabia became, in 2026, a blockade and attacks on Saudi cities and airports. <span class="ev-tag">◇ Analysis</span>
